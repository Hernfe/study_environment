## Sign conventions, to be restated in every recap card

The slides fix these in lecture 1 and everything downstream depends on
them.

- Sign rule 1: ΔV = V_in − V_out, with V_out defined as zero. The
  membrane potential V_m is therefore the inside potential.
- Sign rule 2: outward membrane current is positive. That means positive
  charge leaving the cell, or negative charge entering it.
- Nernst is written both as E_eq = (RT/zF) ln(C_out/C_in) and as
  E_eq = −(RT/zF) ln(C_in/C_out). Both appear on the slides. Make the
  equivalence explicit in the formula explorer, because mixing them up
  is the single most common sign error in this material.

## Lecture 1 formula inventory

Use this as the worked example of how to fill the inventory for later
lectures. Load-bearing unless marked otherwise.

Circuits:
- I = ΔQ/Δt. Current as charge per time. Biological currents pA to µA.
- G = 1/R. Conductance and resistance are reciprocal. Siemens and ohms.
- Ohm's law, ΔV = IR = I/G.
- Series and parallel rules for R, C and G, and Kirchhoff's two laws.
- Voltage divider, V_out = V_in · R2/(R1 + R2), with R2/(R1+R2) named
  the attenuation factor. The slides work an example solving for R1.
- C = q/ΔV = IΔt/ΔV. Capacitance.
- C = εA/d. Geometry of a capacitor. Explains why membrane capacitance
  scales with cell surface area.
- Specific membrane capacitance C_m about 1 µF/cm², treated as a
  constant. This is a `keyNumber`.
- I_C = dq/dt = C dV/dt. Capacitive current flows only while voltage is
  changing. The distinction between dV/dt zero and nonzero is a
  `convention`-grade idea.
- τ_m = R_m · C_m. Membrane time constant.
- V(t) = V_0 (1 − e^(−t/τ_m)) and V(t) = I · R_m (1 − e^(−t/τ_m)).
  Exponential charging.
- f_0 = 1/(2πτ_m) = 1/(2πRC). Corner frequency of the membrane as a
  low-pass filter. The slides give a table of τ against f_0 (1 ms to
  159 Hz, 100 ms to 1.6 Hz) which is good drill material.

Ion movement:
- Fick's first law, J_diff = −D ∂C/∂x.
- Drift, J_drift = −uzC ∂V/∂x.
- Electrodiffusion, J = J_diff + J_drift.
- Stokes-Einstein, D = u RT/F.
- Nernst-Planck, J = −u(RT/F ∂C/∂x + zC ∂V/∂x).
- I = J · zF, converting flux to current.
- Nernst equilibrium potential, both forms above.
- Numerical shortcuts the course uses: E_eq = −58 mV log10(C_in/C_out)
  at 20 C and −61.5 mV log10(C_in/C_out) at 37 C for z = +1, with
  ln(x) = 2.3 log10(x). Put these in the explorer as `variants` and use
  the course's rounded values in solutions, saying which was used.
- Space-charge neutrality, sum of cation charge equals sum of anion
  charge in any volume except within the membrane.
- Donnan equilibrium, (C_out/C_in)^(1/m) = (A_in/A_out)^(1/n), reducing
  at rest with only K+ and Cl- permeant to
  [K_out][Cl_out] = [K_in][Cl_in]. The slides work the 100 mM KCl plus
  50 mM KA example to x ≈ 11, which is a ready-made hard question.

Constants for the recap card: R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), F = 96485 C/equiv
(the slides also use 96500), N_A = 6.03 × 10^23 /mol, e = 1.6022 ×
10^−19 C, T = 293 K at 20 C and 310 K at 37 C.

Reference tables worth reproducing as figures: the ion concentration and
equilibrium potential table for frog muscle, squid axon and a typical
mammalian cell, and the typical mammalian neuron diagram with E_Na, E_K,
E_Cl and E_Ca marked.
