## Formula inventory

Load-bearing: characteristic speed theta = 2 lambda / tau; taught contour x = theta t - lambda/2; lambda = sqrt(Rm a/(2 Ri)); tau = Rm Cm; X = x/lambda, L = l/lambda, T = t/tau; steady infinite V = I ri lambda/2 exp(-abs(x)/lambda); infinite input resistance ri lambda/2, semi-infinite ri lambda and radius power -3/2; steady ODE V'' = V in normalized position; general finite profile V0 [cosh(L-X)+B sinh(L-X)]/[cosh L+B sinh L]; B = Ge/Ginf with Ginf = 1/(ri lambda); matched exp(-X), sealed cosh ratio, open sinh ratio; prescribed endpoint interpolation [V0 sinh(L-X)+Ve sinh X]/sinh L; linear waveform sum at common x,t; annular area pi[(a+h)^2-a^2]; ro = rho_o/area; lambda = sqrt(rm/(ri+ro)). Each introduced numerical relation gets explorer/drill and bank practice. Geometry/per-length formulas are recalled from L03 and assessed again with Exercise 4.

Units: lengths metres, time seconds, voltage volts, resistance ohms, specific membrane resistance ohm m², axial resistivity ohm m, axial resistance per length ohm/m, membrane resistance per length ohm m. Normalize X,L,T to dimensionless quantities. SI internally; source customary units explicitly converted. Passive supplied current positive depolarizes; outward ionic current positive. V is Vm minus Vrest, not absolute resting voltage. Supplied official sheet p4 includes the cable equation, length/time scales, infinite exponential attenuation and characteristic speed. Finite boundary reductions, the affine half-rise contour, input resistance and annular return-path geometry are taught/derived relations, not supplied on that sheet. ## Doubts and corrections

1. PDF2 calls theta a half-rise propagation velocity. The affine half-rise contour is a taught characteristic approximation; passive diffusion does not have a unique all-times wavefront speed. Do not extrapolate its negative time-zero position as a physical front, or equate it to active spike conduction.
2. PDF9 writes a normalized derivative without lambda. Correctly dV/dX = -ri lambda i_axial; dV/dx = -ri i_axial. The zero-slope sealed condition survives this missing scale factor.
3. PDF10 prints sinh(-X)=sinh(X). Correctly sinh is odd, cosh is even. Derivations use the exponential definitions.
4. PDF8/12 graph includes a matched curve beyond L=1. Finite open/sealed curves are evaluated only from 0 through L; comparisons hold V0 fixed, not injected current fixed.
5. PDF11's arbitrary-end formula with Ve is a prescribed two-endpoint representation. Ve and terminal conductance cannot both be chosen independently without a compatible terminal-current condition.
6. PDF14's single-input claim is a typical weak-input statement, not a universal prohibition on one strong synapse crossing threshold. Synaptic conductance changes can break current-source superposition even without voltage-gated channels.
7. PDF18 explicitly flags an incorrectly early trigger-zone response. Retain the warning and ask for causal order; never infer quantitative latency from the schematic or redraw it as measured data.
8. The answer PDF calls the restricted extracellular path's axial resistance a resistivity in its prose. Material resistivities are equal in the example; resistance per length differs because areas differ. Rounded answers are checked against full-precision calculations before using distinct practice values.
