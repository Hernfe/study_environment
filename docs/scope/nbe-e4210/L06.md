### Part 1. Somatic sensation

#### 1. The somatosensory system and skin mechanoreceptors (S7, S8; N 2.1)

- [x] Somatic sensation is everything but hearing, smell, taste, vision and vestibular: touch, temperature, pain, itch, body position. S7. T p. 416 not read; S7 is self-contained.
- [x] Mechanoreceptors: sensitive to physical distortion (bending, stretching); unmyelinated axon terminals; many kinds; good specificity and sensitivity. S8. T p. 417.
- [x] Figure 12.1: Merkel's disk, free nerve ending, Meissner's corpuscle, hair follicle receptor, Pacinian corpuscle, Ruffini's ending; hairy and glabrous skin; epidermis and dermis. S8. T p. 417.
- [x] Depth and size: Pacinian deep in dermis, up to 2 mm; Ruffini slightly smaller; Meissner one tenth Pacinian, in the dermal ridges of glabrous skin; Merkel in the epidermis, nerve terminal plus a non-neural Merkel cell. T p. 417.

#### 2. Receptive fields, sensitivity and specificity (S9, S10; N 2.1)

- [x] Receptive field mapped by recording one median nerve afferent while probing the skin (Vallbo and Johansson, 1984). S9. T pp. 417-418.
- [x] Small fields: Meissner and Merkel (a few mm). Large fields: Pacinian and Ruffini (whole finger, half the palm). S9, S10 table. T p. 418.
- [x] Rapidly adapting: Meissner, Pacinian. Slowly adapting: Merkel, Ruffini. S10 table. T p. 418.
- [x] Frequency tuning: Pacinian best at 200-300 Hz, Meissner around 50 Hz (texture; 1-10 Hz flutter). Lower threshold means more sensitive. S10 graph. T p. 419.
- [x] Submodalities: Merkel sustained pressure and edges; Meissner skin motion, flutter; Pacinian high-frequency vibration; Ruffini skin stretch. N 2.1 table (notes cite [1] for Ruffini stretch). Hair follicle receptors: bending a hair deforms the follicle, either adaptation type. T p. 419.

#### 3. Adaptation and the Pacinian capsule (S11; N 2.2)

- [x] Compression transfers energy to the terminal; mechanosensitive channels open; depolarizing receptor potential. S11. T pp. 419-420.
- [x] Capsule layers slip during maintained pressure, so the terminal is no longer deformed; release deforms it again (offset response). S11. T p. 420.
- [x] Stripped corpuscle: still responds, much more prolonged response; the capsule makes it rapidly adapting, not mechanosensitive. S11. T pp. 419-420.
- [x] HW3 2B.2: intact Pacinian versus Merkel for a held probe; little Pacinian firing during the hold does not mean the probe is gone. Demo and quiz.

#### 4. Mechanosensitive ion channels (S12; N 2.2)

- [x] Force by membrane stretch, by links to extracellular proteins, or by links to the cytoskeleton (actin, microtubules). S12. T p. 420.
- [x] Indirect route: second messengers (DAG, IP3). S12. T p. 420.
- Piezo2 in Merkel cells (T p. 420): not on the slide; one line at most, not quizzed. Left out.

#### 5. Two-point discrimination (S12; N 2.2)

- [x] Varies at least 20-fold; Braille dots 1 mm high, 2.5 mm apart, about 600 letters per minute. S12. T p. 421.
- [x] Why the fingertip wins: receptor density, small receptive fields (Merkel), more brain tissue per mm of skin, possibly special mechanisms. S12. T p. 422. HW3 2B.1.

#### 6. Afferent axons I to IV (S13, S14; N 2.3)

- [x] Afferent towards the CNS, efferent away. S13.
- [x] Primary afferents enter through dorsal roots; cell bodies in dorsal root ganglia. S13. T p. 422.
- [x] Figure 12.10 table: A-alpha / I, 13-20 µm, 80-120 m/s, muscle proprioceptors; A-beta / II, 6-12 µm, 35-75 m/s, skin mechanoreceptors; A-delta / III, 1-5 µm, 5-30 m/s, pain, temperature; C / IV, 0.2-1.5 µm, 0.5-2 m/s, temperature, pain, itch. C unmyelinated, the rest myelinated. S14. T pp. 422-423.
- [x] First and second pain from A-delta and C speeds. N 2.3. T p. 442. Detail block only (D3).

#### 7. Spinal segments and dermatomes (S15, S16; N 3.1)

- [x] 30 segments in four groups: C1-8, T1-12, L1-5, S1-5. S15. T p. 423.
- [x] Inner grey core, thick white matter tracts (columns); dorsal horn, intermediate zone, ventral horn. S15. T p. 426.
- [x] Dermatome: skin supplied by the left and right dorsal roots of one segment; bands; best seen on all fours. S16. T pp. 423-424.
- [x] Neighbouring dermatomes overlap: one cut root does not abolish sensation; three adjacent roots must be cut. N 3.1. T p. 424.
- [x] Dorsal roots in, ventral roots out, mixed spinal nerve. N 3.1. T pp. 422, 457. HW3 3A.3.
- Shingles (Box 12.1), cauda equina and lumbar puncture: out (box; not on slides).

#### 8. Dorsal column-medial lemniscal pathway (S17; N 3.2)

- [x] Touch and vibration take a different path from pain and temperature. S17. T p. 426.
- [x] A-beta axon branches: one to the deep dorsal horn (reflexes), one up the ipsilateral dorsal column without a synapse to the dorsal column nuclei. S17 Figure 12.14. T pp. 426-428.
- [x] Dorsal column nuclei at the spinal cord-medulla junction; second-order axons decussate in the medulla; medial lemniscus; VP nucleus of the thalamus; S1. S17 Figure 12.15. T p. 428.
- [x] Relay nuclei transform information. T p. 428. One line.
- [x] HW3 2A.1: right index finger to the left S1, route named. Quiz.
- Lateral inhibition (Box 12.2): out.

#### 9. Trigeminal touch pathway (S18; N 3.2)

- [x] HW3 2A.2: cheek touch does not enter through a spinal root. Quiz.
- Facial, glossopharyngeal and vagus contributions: one line at most. Left out.

#### 10. Somatosensory cortex, area 3b, layers and columns (S19, S20, S24; N 4.1)

- [x] Area 3b is S1: dense VP input, responsive to somatosensory stimuli, lesions impair, stimulation evokes sensation. S19. T p. 430.
- [x] 3a body position; 3b to 1 texture, 3b to 2 size and shape. T p. 431. Detail block.
- [x] Layered; thalamic input to layer IV; columns (Mountcastle first described them in S1); alternating rapidly and slowly adapting columns within a finger. S20. T p. 431.
- [x] Brodmann map reminder: areas 1, 2, 3 S1; area 4 M1. S24. Text only.

#### 11. The somatotopic map (S21, S22; N 4.1, 4.2)

- [x] Size follows receptor density and importance (lips, tongue, fingers). T pp. 432-433. HW3 2B.3.
- [x] S22: finger maps in area 3b by fMRI and optical imaging in monkeys (Chen et al. 2007); both indirect, haemodynamic. N 4.2. One line plus a detail.

#### 12. Map plasticity (S23; N 4.2)

#### 13. Posterior parietal cortex (S25; N 4.3)

#### 14. Pain and nociceptors (S26; N 5.1)

- [x] Nociceptors: free nerve endings; pain is the perception, nociception the mechanism. S26. T pp. 437-438.
- [x] Many types; C and A-delta fibres; polymodal (mechanical, thermal, chemical). S26. T p. 439.
- [x] Hyperalgesia; inflammatory soup (bradykinin, prostaglandins, histamine, substance P, K+) modulates excitability. S26 Figure 12.25. T pp. 439-441.

#### 15. Spinothalamic pathway (S27; N 5.1)

- [x] Second-order axons decussate immediately and ascend in the spinothalamic tract; pain and temperature ascend contralaterally. S27. T p. 444.
- [x] Thalamus: VP and intralaminar nuclei; wider cortical territory. S27 Figure 12.30 label. T pp. 445-446.
- [x] Figure 12.31: touch ipsilateral in the cord, pain contralateral; hemisection gives touch, vibration, position loss on the same side below the lesion and pain and temperature loss on the opposite side. S27. T p. 445. N 5.1.

#### 16. Pain regulation (S28; N 5.2)

- [x] Low-threshold mechanoreceptor activity reduces pain; gate theory (Figure 12.33): projection neuron excited by A-beta and C, inhibitory interneuron excited by A-beta, inhibited by C. S28. T pp. 446-447.
- [x] PAG stimulation gives profound analgesia; PAG to raphe nuclei (serotonin) to dorsal horn. S28 Figure 12.34. T pp. 446-448.

#### 17. Opioids and endorphins (S29; N 5.2)

- [x] Endogenous morphine-like substances, endorphins; bind tightly and specifically to opioid receptors; widespread, concentrated in nociceptive areas. S29. T p. 448.
- [x] Mechanism: less glutamate release, postsynaptic hyperpolarization. T p. 448. N 5.2.
- [x] Corder et al. 2018: affective, cognitive and sensory circuits; descending modulation via PAG and rostral ventromedial medulla. S29. Text only.
- Placebo (Box 12.6), drug side effects: out (one line at most).

#### 18. Temperature (S30, S31; N 5.3)

- [x] Thermoreceptors exquisitely sensitive; hypothalamus and spinal cord for physiological responses; skin receptors for perception. S30. T p. 449.
- [x] TRP channels: TRPA1, TRPM8 (menthol, below 25 °C), TRPV4, TRPV3, TRPV1 (capsaicin, above 43 °C), TRPV2; one channel type per neuron; labelled line ("the CNS does not know what kind of stimulus caused the receptor to fire"). S30. T pp. 449-450.
- [x] Adaptation: cooling 38 to 32 °C bursts the cold receptor and silences the warm one, both adapt; reverse on rewarming. S31. T pp. 449-450.
