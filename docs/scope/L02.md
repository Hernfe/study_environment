### 1. Membrane and protein foundations (N 1.1-1.3, S 5-10)

- [x] Phospholipid: polar head with phosphate (hydrophilic), nonpolar hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic). Bilayer, tails inside, heads facing the watery in and out. Barrier to water-soluble ions. S6 (Figure 3.3). N 1.1. T pp. 59-60.
- [x] Cytosol versus extracellular fluid; ions carry charge; spheres of hydration; cation, anion, monovalent, divalent. T pp. 57-59. FLAG: hydration shell is on S34 (Figure 4.9) and in N 1.4; the rest is textbook context for the slide-5 list. Kept short.
- [x] Membrane potential: voltage difference between inside and outside; inside negative at rest; resting membrane potential. S5 title, S14-16. N 1.1. T pp. 57, 66-67.
- [x] Proteins are synthesized by ribosomes; amino acids joined by peptide bonds (Figure 3.5). S7, S9. N 1.2. T pp. 60-62.
- [x] Free ribosomes make cytosolic proteins; rough-ER ribosomes make membrane and secreted proteins; Golgi sorts; most axonal proteins made in the soma and transported. S7-8 (Figure 2.8, bonus homework Ch 2 pp. 29-39). N 1.2. T pp. 36-39. Cross-link to L01.
- [x] Mitochondria supply the ATP for pumps; motor proteins move vesicles on microtubules. N 1.2. T pp. 38-39. FLAG: notes only as a link back to L01; one line.
- [ ] Local translation of selected mRNAs in axons and terminals (Jung 2012, Hafner 2019). N 1.2 external refs. FLAG: notes only, not on slides. Left out.
- [x] 20 amino acids; R groups differ; hydrophilic versus hydrophobic side chains (Figure 3.4). S9. N 1.3. T pp. 60-61.
- [x] Scales of protein structure: primary (sequence), secondary (alpha helix), tertiary (3D fold of one chain), quaternary (subunits) (Figure 3.6). S10. N 1.3. T pp. 60-62.
- [x] Membrane protein: hydrophobic regions in the bilayer, hydrophilic regions facing the fluids or lining a water-filled pore. Structure determines function; one amino acid change can disrupt a channel (Weaver mouse, T p. 74). N 1.3. T pp. 62-63.

### 2. Ion channels as gated pores (N 1.4, S 11)

- [x] Ion channel: four to six subunits around a central pore (Figure 3.7, five subunits). S11. N 1.4. T p. 63.
- [x] Ion selectivity (pore diameter and R groups lining it); gating (opened and closed by the local environment). N 1.4. T p. 63.
- [x] Selectivity filter versus gate: the filter decides which ion, the gate decides whether the pore is open. N 1.4, N 4.5. T pp. 63, 93-94.
- [x] Hydration shell: an ion cannot cross the bilayer without losing it; a channel is a water-filled path. N 1.4. T pp. 58, 94 (Figure 4.9 on S34).
- [x] Ion pumps: membrane proteins that use ATP to move ions against their gradients. S20. N 1.1, 3.4. T pp. 63-64.

### 3. Electrochemical gradients and ionic current (N 2.1, S 12-13)

- [x] Diffusion: movement down the concentration gradient; concentration c = n/V (molar). S12 (Figure 3.8). N 2.1. T pp. 64-65, Box 3.1.
- [x] Diffusion across the membrane needs channels permeable to the ion and a concentration gradient. S12. N 2.1. T p. 64.
- [x] Electrical field: cations move to the cathode, anions to the anode; current I is positive in the direction of cation movement (Figure 3.9, 3.10). S13. N 2.1. T pp. 64-66.
- [x] Voltage V, conductance g (siemens), resistance R = 1/g, Ohm's law I = gV. No channels means g = 0 and no current even with a large voltage. T p. 65. N 2.1.
- [x] Electrochemical gradient: concentration and electrical forces, same or opposite direction. N 2.1. T pp. 65-68.
- [x] Ionic current I_ion = g_ion (Vm - E_ion); driving force Vm - E_ion; g depends on number of open channels. N 2.1, N 3.3. T pp. 68, 88-89. Learning objective 2.

### 4. Resting membrane potential and the Nernst equation (N 3.1-3.2, S 14-18)

- [x] Measuring Vm: microelectrode inside, ground wire outside, voltmeter; about -65 mV (Figure 3.11). S14-15. N 3.1. T pp. 66-67. Self-check 1 (what -65 mV means).
- [x] Resting potential arises from selective permeabilities. S14-16 title bullet.
- [x] K+ equilibrium: K+ concentrated inside, K+ channels let it out, inside goes negative, electrical force pulls it back, equilibrium at EK about -80 mV (Figure 3.12). S14. N 3.1. T pp. 67-68.
- [x] Na+ equilibrium: Na+ concentrated outside, Na+ channels let it in, inside goes positive, ENa positive (Figure 3.14). S15. N 3.1. T p. 69.
- [x] Four points (S16, T p. 68): (1) large voltage changes from minuscule concentration changes (100 to 99.99999 mM); (2) charge sits at the membrane surfaces, membrane = capacitor; (3) ions move at a rate proportional to Vm - E_ion (driving force); (4) E_ion can be calculated from the concentration difference. N 3.2. Figure 3.13.
- [x] Nernst equation: E_ion = 2.303 (RT/zF) log([ion]o/[ion]i); R, T, z, F, log10, o, i defined (Box 3.2). S17-18. N 3.2. T pp. 69-70.
- [x] At 37 C: E = 61.54 mV / z * log(out/in); 30.77 mV for Ca2+. Worked example 1:20 gives log = -1.3, EK = -80 mV. S18. T Box 3.2. HW1 exercise 5 (100/5 and 100/20 mM).
- [x] Nernst has no permeability term; E_ion exists even when channels are closed; E is the potential that just balances the gradient so no net current flows. S18 text. N 3.2. T Box 3.2.
- [x] Temperature and charge: E proportional to T, inversely proportional to z. T Box 3.2. FLAG: textbook derivation note; used for the calculator's valence slider only.

### 5. Key neuronal ions and pumps (N 3.3-3.4, S 19-20)

- [x] Figure 3.15 table: K+ 5 out / 100 in, 1:20, -80 mV; Na+ 150 / 15, 10:1, +62 mV; Ca2+ 2 / 0.0002, 10000:1, +123 mV; Cl- 150 / 13, 11.5:1, -65 mV. S19, repeated on S31 and S37. N 3.3. T p. 71.
- [x] E_ion is the potential the membrane would reach if selectively permeable to that ion; no net current at E_ion or when no channels are open. N 3.3. T p. 71.
- [x] At rest Vm is close to EK; ECl is near rest so opening Cl- channels changes Vm little but stabilises it. N 3.3. T pp. 71-73. FLAG: Cl- stabilisation is notes plus textbook; one line.
- [x] Sodium-potassium pump: ATP-driven, exchanges internal Na+ for external K+, against gradients; 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in per cycle (notes); up to 70 percent of brain ATP (textbook). "Without ion pumps the resting potential would not exist" (quote on S20). S20 (Figure 3.16). N 3.4. T pp. 71-72.
- [x] Pump moves one net positive charge out per cycle, a small direct contribution; its main job is the gradients. N 3.4. FLAG: notes only; one line.
- [x] Calcium pump and calcium-binding proteins and organelles keep cytosolic Ca2+ at 0.0002 mM. N 3.4. T p. 71.
- [x] Channels, not pumps, make the fast voltage changes of the action potential; the pump restores gradients over time (misconception). N 3.4, N 6.3. T pp. 98-99. HW1 exercise 3, neuron C (pumps blocked, gradients fade slowly).

### 6. The Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation (N 3.5, S 21-23)

- [x] GHK voltage equation: Em = (RT/F) ln( sum P_M [M]out + sum P_A [A]in ) / ( sum P_M [M]in + sum P_A [A]out ) for monovalent cations M and anions A; P = permeability; anions reversed. S21-22. N 3.5.
- [x] K, Na, Cl form: Em = (RT/F) ln( PNa[Na]o + PK[K]o + PCl[Cl]i ) / ( PNa[Na]i + PK[K]i + PCl[Cl]o ). Nernst-like, one term per permeant ion; with one ion it reduces to Nernst. S22, S31. N 3.5.
- [x] Textbook two-ion form at 37 C: Vm = 61.54 mV log( PK[K]o + PNa[Na]o ) / ( PK[K]i + PNa[Na]i ). PK:PNa = 40:1 with Figure 3.15 gives 61.54 log(350/4015) = -65 mV (Box 3.3). S23. N 3.5. T pp. 72-73.
- [x] Real neurons: -65 mV, not -80 mV, because of a steady Na+ leak; Vm lies between EK and ENa, closer to EK. S23. N 3.5. T p. 72. Self-check 2.
- [x] Raising an ion's permeability moves Vm toward that ion's E; raising PNa depolarises, raising PK moves toward EK. N 3.5. T pp. 90-91.
- [x] Nernst answers "one ion's equilibrium potential"; GHK answers "Vm from several ions' concentrations and relative permeabilities". N 3.5, N 6.1. Self-check 3. Learning objective 3.
- [x] Conductance g (open channels carrying current) versus permeability P (how readily an ion crosses; used by GHK). N 4.3. FLAG: notes only; one compare row.
- [x] Sensitivity of Vm to [K+]o: 5 to 50 mM changes Vm from -65 to -17 mV (Figure 3.19); depolarization defined. T p. 75 (assigned reading, S24). HW1 exercises 3B and 5.
- [x] Regulation of [K+]o: blood-brain barrier, astrocyte potassium spatial buffering (Figure 3.20); muscle and heart are not protected (Box 3.5, KCl injection). T pp. 76-78 (assigned reading, S24). FLAG: assigned reading only, kept in a detail block, quizzed only through the [K+]o effect.
- [x] Potassium channels: four subunits, pore loop forms the selectivity filter; Shaker fly, scorpion toxin, MacKinnon 2003 Nobel; Weaver mouse single amino acid mutation lets Na+ through. T pp. 73-75 (assigned reading, S24). FLAG: assigned reading only; detail block, not quizzed beyond the pore loop.

### 7. Action potential waveform and intracellular recording (N 4.1, S 26-28)

- [x] Intracellular recording: microelectrode inside, ground, amplifier, oscilloscope; extracellular recording sees a small biphasic signal in microvolts (Box 4.1 Figure A). S26-28. N 4.1. T p. 83.

### 8. Threshold, all-or-none firing and rate coding (N 4.2, S 29-30)

- [x] Two electrodes at the axon hillock, one stimulating (injected positive charge), one recording (Figure 4.2). S29. N 4.2. T pp. 84-85.
- [x] Threshold: below it no spike, above it a full spike; all-or-none; the spike is a binary discrete event. S30. N 4.2. T p. 84.
- [x] Firing rate increases with depolarizing current (Figure 4.3); rate is not binary; firing frequency encodes stimulus intensity; about 1 Hz at threshold up to 50 Hz and more. S30. N 4.2. T p. 85.
- [x] Maximum about 1000 Hz; absolute refractory period about 1 ms; relative refractory period, more current needed for several ms. T pp. 85-86. S37.
- [x] A stronger stimulus does not make a spike taller; it makes the first spike earlier or the rate higher (misconception). N 6.3.
- [ ] Optogenetics: channelrhodopsin-2, halorhodopsin (Box 4.2). T pp. 86-88. Not on slides. Left out.

### 9. Conductances during the action potential (N 4.3, S 31)

- [x] Idealized neuron with pumps, K+ channels, Na+ channels; EK = -80, ENa = +62 (Figure 4.5). T pp. 88-89. N 4.3.
- [x] IK = gK (Vm - EK); current flows only when gK > 0 and Vm differs from EK; current takes Vm toward E_ion. T pp. 88-90. N 2.1, 4.3.
- [x] Driving force on Na+ at rest is large, -80 - 62 = -142 mV, but no current without gNa. T p. 90.
- [x] Figure 4.6 sequence: (a) gK >> gNa, Vm = EK; (b) gNa >> gK, Na+ influx takes Vm toward ENa (rising phase); (c) gK >> gNa, K+ efflux takes Vm back toward EK (falling phase); (d) rest restored. S31. N 4.3. T pp. 90-91.
- [x] GHK links the same story to permeabilities: switching the dominant permeability flips Vm. S31 shows the K, Na, Cl GHK form beside Figure 4.6. N 4.3.

### 10. Voltage clamp and the Hodgkin-Huxley model (N 4.4, S 32)

- [x] Voltage clamp (Kenneth C. Cole): hold Vm at a chosen value, measure the current needed; deduce conductance changes at different voltages. S32. N 4.4. T p. 92.
- [x] Hodgkin and Huxley, Cambridge, around 1950, squid giant axon: rising phase = transient increase in gNa and Na+ influx; falling phase = increase in gK and K+ efflux. Nobel Prize 1963 (with Eccles). S32 (text and Nobel annual cover). N 4.4. T p. 92, p. 101.
- [x] 1952 quantitative model of how gNa and gK change with voltage and time (Hodgkin and Huxley 1952). N 4.4 external ref 4. FLAG: notes only for the date and the word "model"; one line.
- [x] HH gates: activated by depolarization, inactivated at positive potentials, deinactivated only after return to negative. T p. 92. Links to S36.
- [x] Separating the currents: TTX blocks Na+ channels (T p. 96); ion substitution or channel blockers separate overlapping currents (N 6.2). FLAG: the demo also offers a K+ channel blocker labelled TEA, which is not named on the slides or the cited textbook pages; kept as a control label only, not quizzed.
- [x] Early inward current and delayed outward current under a depolarizing step (self-check 4). N 6.5-6.6. T pp. 97-99.

### 11. Channel structure and voltage sensing (N 4.5, S 33-34)

- [x] Voltage-gated sodium channel: one long polypeptide, four domains I-IV, each with six transmembrane alpha helices S1-S6; domains clump to form the pore (Figure 4.7). S33. N 4.5. T pp. 92-93.
- [x] Pore loops between S5 and S6 form the selectivity filter; twelve times more permeable to Na+ than K+; partially hydrated Na+ fits (0.5 nm), hydrated K+ does not (Figure 4.9). S34. N 4.5. T pp. 92-94.
- [x] S4 is the voltage sensor: positively charged residues along the helix; depolarization twists S4 and opens the gate; closed at -65 mV, open at -40 mV (Figure 4.8). S33-34. N 4.5. T p. 94.
- [x] Voltage-gated potassium channels: four separate subunits, open about 1 ms after depolarization, delayed rectifier. T pp. 97-98. N 4.7. FLAG: structure not on slides beyond the assigned reading; the 1 ms delay is on S37 (Figure 4.12).

### 12. Patch clamp (N 4.6, S 35)

- [x] Patch clamp (Sakmann and Neher, Nobel 1991): pipette tip 1-5 um sealed to a membrane patch; gigaohm seal isolates one or a few channels; patch can be torn off; currents measured at a clamped voltage (Box 4.3). S35. N 4.6. T pp. 94-95.
- [x] Single-channel record: closed = no current, open = a discrete current step; amplitude gives the single-channel current (conductance = current / driving force), duration the open time; channels flip between two states; unitary conductance; over a million ions per second. N 4.6. T p. 95.
- [x] Openings are probabilistic; repeated steps give open probability; population current = single-channel current x number x open probability. N 4.6. FLAG: notes only; one line in a detail block.
- [x] Three methods compared: intracellular recording (measures Vm, injects current), voltage clamp (holds Vm, measures total current), patch clamp (single channels). N 6.2. Learning objective 5.

### 13. Sodium channel states and refractoriness (N 4.7, S 36-37)

- [x] Four stages: 1 closed; 2 opening at depolarization with little delay, for at most 1 ms; 3 inactivation (stays closed while depolarized); 4 deinactivation at hyperpolarization (return to -65 mV). Ball-and-chain model (Figure 4.10). S36. N 4.7. T pp. 94-96.
- [x] Channel properties explain the spike: threshold (channels open at a critical depolarization), fast rise (rapid opening), brevity (1 ms open time), absolute refractory period (inactivation). T pp. 94-95.
- [x] Figure 4.12 layers: Vm; three single Na+ channel currents; summed Na+ current (inward); three single K+ channel currents; summed K+ current (outward, delayed, longer); net transmembrane current. S37. N 4.7. T pp. 98-99.
- [x] Molecular basis, point by point: threshold, rising phase, overshoot (near ENa), falling phase (Na+ inactivation plus K+ channels opening), undershoot (extra gK takes Vm toward EK), absolute refractory period (Na+ channels inactivated), relative refractory period (K+ channels still open, more current needed). S37. N 4.7. T p. 98. HW1 exercise 4A-4E.
- [x] Channelopathy: generalized epilepsy with febrile seizures, slowed Na+ inactivation. T p. 96. FLAG: textbook only; one line in a detail block.
- [x] HW1 exercise 3 and 4F: Na+ channels cannot open, resting Vm normal but no spikes (neuron A); high [K+]o depolarises rest (neuron B); pump block, gradients fade slowly, both fail with time (neuron C). HW1. Content sections 6, 13; quiz.

- [x] Sensory neurons: spike-initiation zone at the sensory nerve ending; spikes travel toward the CNS. S38. N 5.1. T p. 105.

### 15. Regenerative conduction (N 5.2, S 39)

- [x] Conduction: Na+ entry depolarises the membrane just ahead to threshold; its channels open; repeated along the axon; fuse analogy; no decrement (Figure 4.13). S39. N 5.2. T p. 100.
- [x] One direction because the membrane behind is refractory (Na+ channels inactivated); orthodromic (soma to terminal) versus antidromic (experimental) conduction; an axon can conduct either way if stimulated in the middle. N 5.2. T p. 100. HW1 exercise 2C.
- [x] Typical velocity 10 m/s; 2 ms spike occupies 2 cm of axon. T p. 101. FLAG: textbook only; used as a key number.

### 16. Axon diameter and myelin (N 5.3, no L02 slide)

- [x] Two paths for current: down the axon or out across the membrane (leaky hose); wide axon and few open pores send current farther, so faster conduction; velocity rises with diameter; squid giant axon 1 mm. N 5.3. T p. 101. FLAG: no L02 slide; notes section 5.3 cites the textbook only. Kept because the notes include it as a numbered section with a learning objective and a self-check question, and myelin and nodes of Ranvier were on the L01 slides.
- [x] Myelin insulates between nodes, reduces leakage, current spreads farther and faster; Schwann cells (PNS) and oligodendroglia (CNS); nodes of Ranvier 0.2-2.0 mm apart with concentrated Na+ channels; saltatory conduction (Figures 4.14, 4.15). N 5.3. T pp. 103-104. Self-check 5 (demyelination slows or blocks conduction). HW1 exercise 2 (structure C is a myelinated axon).
- [x] Small axons need more depolarization to reach threshold and are blocked first by local anaesthetics (lidocaine binds the S6 helix from inside the open pore). T pp. 101-102 (Box 4.4). FLAG: textbook only; detail block.
- [x] Multiple sclerosis and Guillain-Barre: demyelination slows or blocks conduction. T p. 103 (Box 4.5). FLAG: textbook only; used as the applied example for self-check 5.
